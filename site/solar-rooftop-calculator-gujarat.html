How the calculation works

  1. Size: yearly units ÷ yield of your city, limited by roof area (about 100 sq ft per kW) and sanctioned load, rounded down to 0.5 kW.
  2. Generation: spread over 12 months using a Gujarat seasonal profile (lower in the monsoon).
  3. Bill after solar: each month, solar units are netted against your use and the remaining units go through the full DISCOM tariff. Surplus is credited at ₹2.25/unit.
  4. Subsidy: PM Surya Ghar: ₹30,000/kW for the first 2 kW, ₹18,000 for the 3rd kW, maximum ₹78,000. Details.
  5. Over 25 years: tariffs rise and panels age at the rates in Assumptions; payback, IRR and lifetime savings come from that cash flow.

Annual yield estimates (kWh per kW installed, after typical system losses) are approximations for south-facing fixed-tilt rooftop systems. Refine per city with PVGIS or NASA POWER data.

Frequently asked questions

How many kW of solar do I need?

Divide your yearly units by about 1,450–1,550 (what 1 kW of panels makes in a year in Gujarat). A home using 300 units a month (3,600 a year) needs about 2 kW. Net metering is allowed up to your sanctioned load.

What is the payback period of rooftop solar in Gujarat?

Usually 2.5–5 years after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. For example, a 2 kW system on a UGVCL home using 300 units a month costs about ₹50,000 after subsidy and saves about ₹22,423 in the first year, a payback of 2.2 years. Bigger bills pay back faster because solar replaces the costliest slabs first.

Why does this calculator give different savings from installer calculators?

Most calculators multiply units by a flat ₹/unit. This one recalculates your bill slab by slab, with fixed charges, FPPPA and duty, before and after solar, so the saving reflects the slabs you actually drop out of.

What happens to extra solar units?

Under net metering, units you export offset units you import in the same billing cycle. Any surplus left over is bought by the DISCOM at about ₹2.25/unit, so oversizing beyond your use earns little.
