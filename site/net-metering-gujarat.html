Net Metering in Gujarat

What happens to the solar units you send to the grid, and why sizing to your own use gives the best return.

How it works

A bi-directional (net) meter records units you import from the grid and units your panels export. In each billing cycle, exports are set off against imports, and you pay only for the net units through the normal slab tariff.

Surplus units

If you export more than you import in a billing cycle, the DISCOM buys the surplus at about ₹2.25/unit (verify) for residential consumers. Each unit of your own use that solar replaces is worth ₹6–9 because it removes your highest-slab units, FPPPA and duty, so a system sized close to your yearly consumption gives the best payback.

Capacity limit

Residential rooftop systems can be installed up to your sanctioned load. If you want a bigger system, apply for a load increase first.

Things that change your savings

  • Monsoon months (June to September) generate 25–40% less.
  • Shade from water tanks, parapets or nearby buildings cuts output sharply.
  • Fixed charges and meter rent stay on the bill even if your net units are zero.
